Control Your Computer
With Gestures

AirPoint is a mouse for people who cannot hold one. A webcam watches the hand; signs the hand can actually make become clicks, drags and scrolls. It measures a tremor and filters it out, learns each person's own signs, and works by pointing alone, by face, or by voice when signs are too much.

System Architecture

End-to-End Pipeline

From webcam frame to OS mouse event at ~30 FPS on a laptop CPU — no GPU, no video leaves the machine. Two brains, one switch: a trained MLP reads the hand shape; a state machine turns shapes over time into actions.

Version 2

Beyond the six signs

The same pipeline, opened up for bodies and needs the six signs do not fit. Each part is built and covered by tests; what has been measured, and on what, is stated under Results. What still needs people is said there too.

Fits your body
A one-minute calibration finds your comfortable reach, the signs your hand can and cannot make, and how much it shakes. Either hand. A pause sign, hints, sounds.
Tremor measured, then cancelled
A meter finds the tremor's frequency; a notch filter removes that band before smoothing. Plain smoothing cannot: it has to lag to hide a regular shake.
Pointing alone
Hold still to click, or open a wheel at the cursor and pick right click, drag, scroll, double click, pause or talk by direction. No sign needed.
Face mode
The head moves the cursor, an open mouth clicks, raised eyebrows right-click. Everything after the tracker is shared with the hand.
Teach a sign, speak for me
Hold a new sign for eight seconds and say what it does: speak a phrase aloud, press keys, type, open an app, pause, start dictation, or run an errand.
Talk
One tap starts listening and the same tap ends it. The system's own dictation types; an optional on-laptop engine also takes spoken commands.
AI helper
A whole errand in one request. Each step is checked, shown at the cursor and stoppable with a fist; anything that cannot be undone waits for a yes. Off until switched on: it is the one part that sends anything off the laptop.
In a browser
The core, ported to JavaScript and held to the Python code by a parity fixture, so anyone can try it from a QR code with nothing installed.
